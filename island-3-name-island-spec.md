# Island 3 — "Name Island" — Full Design Specification

**Sea:** 1 (Calm Sea, Pre-A1) · **Position:** first standard-template island · **Est. play time:** 35–50 min across 3–5 sessions
**Grammar focus:** the copula (*I am / you are / this is*) — the #1 Hebrew-transfer error target
**Prerequisites:** Island 0 (Harbor tutorial, loanwords); Islands 1–2 (Letter Coves) for Readers mode only

---

## 1. Learning Objectives (Can-Do Shields)

| Shield | Can-do statement | Evidence collected |
|---|---|---|
| 🛡️ Greeter | Can greet and say goodbye appropriately | 3+ correct uses of hello/hi/bye in activities + boss turn 1 |
| 🛡️ My Name Is | Can ask for and give a name ("What's your name?" / "I'm ___") | Boss turns 2–3 |
| 🛡️ How Are You | Can ask and answer "How are you?" with a feeling word | Boss turn 4 + Level 5 chunk assembly |
| 🛡️ Bridge Builder | Uses am/are in simple sentences (copula not dropped) | ≥80% copula-inclusion in Level 5 & boss |

## 2. Content Inventory

**New vocabulary (18 items):**
hello, hi, goodbye, bye, good morning, boy, girl, friend, teacher, man, woman, name, yes, no, please, thank you, happy, sad

**Chunks (7):**
1. What's your name?
2. My name is ___ / I'm ___
3. How are you?
4. I'm fine, thank you!
5. Nice to meet you!
6. This is my friend.
7. I'm happy / I'm sad.

**Grammar target:** copula *am/are/is* as an unskippable element — taught implicitly through the **Bridge Plank mechanic** (see §4, Level 5), with one Hebrew hint card in Readers mode.

**Review injection (SRS):** every level draws 20–30% of its items from Island 0 loanwords (pizza, telephone, banana...) and, in Readers mode, letter recognition from the Coves. Items surface according to their SRS due-date; if nothing is due, recently-learned items fill the quota.

## 3. Island Map Screen

The child's ship docks at a beach village. Visible: 7 level markers along a winding path (numbered footprint signs), the Trading Post ship at the dock, Captain Morgan's hut (boss, initially fogged), a mastery meter shaped as a flag rising up a mast (fills with SRS "Known" percentage), and the parrot companion who names anything tapped ("Beach! ## 4. Level-by-Level Design

### Level 1 — "Meet the Islanders" (Listen & Tap)
*Skill: receptive vocabulary · Pedagogy: dual coding, comprehensible input*
Six villagers stand on the beach (boy, girl, man, woman, teacher, friend-pair). Intro pass: each character waves as narrator says the word twice (normal speed, then slow). Practice pass: "Tap the girl!" — 12 prompts, items interleaved, each item retrieved ≥2 times. Errors: character shakes head kindly, target character glows + word replays, retry immediately (no penalty), item re-queued for end of level.
Second half adds greetings: characters say "Hello!" / "Good morning!" and the child taps the matching speech-bubble icon (sun icon = good morning, waving hand = hello, walking-away = goodbye).
**Exit criterion:** all 10 new items answered correctly at least once unassisted. Coins: 10 (+1 per first-try item).

### Level 2 — "Beach Pairs" (Picture Match)
*Skill: recognition → recall · Pedagogy: retrieval practice*
Memory-pairs grid, 2 rounds. Round 1 (recognition): flip cards to match picture ↔ picture while audio names each flip. Round 2 (retrieval): match **audio shell** (plays word when tapped) ↔ picture — the child must hold the form in memory. Readers mode round 3: match written word ↔ picture (words limited to decodable spellings taught in Letter Coves: boy, girl, man; sight-words hello/bye taught as whole shapes with a star marker meaning "camera word — remember its picture").
**SRS effect:** each correct unassisted match promotes the item one state.

### Level 3A (Readers) — "Message in a Bottle" (Word Builder)
Letter tiles wash ashore in bottles; the child drags them to spell 6 words (boy, girl, man, hi, yes, no — all decodable). Each placed letter plays its sound; completed word plays whole-word audio + the character appears. Wrong slot: tile hops back gently, target slot pulses. Hebrew instruction line: "בנו את המילה מהאותיות" with audio-free icon fallback.

### Level 3B (Pre-Readers) — "Parrot Ears" (Sound Match)
Phonological awareness without letters: (a) "Which one starts like *b-b-boy*?" — pick from 3 pictures; (b) syllable-clap: parrot claps twice for "hello", child taps the drum the same number of times; (c) minimal-pair warmup: happy/hello discrimination ("Point to *happy*!"). Seeds the /h/ and /b/ sounds later needed for reading.

### Level 4 — "Simon the Parrot Says" (Listen & Do, TPR)
The avatar stands in the village; spoken commands move it: "Walk to the teacher!", "Wave to the girl!", "Say hello to the boy!" (tapping the boy triggers avatar's cheerful "Hello!"), "Jump!", "Sit down!". 10 commands, complexity ramps to two-step: "Walk to the woman and say good morning!". No text on screen in either mode — pure listening. Errors: parrot repeats slowly with pointing gesture; never marked wrong, just "let's try together!"
**Why it matters:** this is the TPR anchor level; research shows listen-and-act builds confidence pre-production.

### Level 5 — "The Bridge" (Chunk Assembly) ⭐ the copula level
The pirate must cross plank bridges between rocks. Each bridge = a sentence; word tiles float in the water; the child drags them into slots. **The copula tile (am/are/is) is rendered as a wooden plank — without it there is literally a gap in the bridge and the pirate cannot cross.** Sentences: *I am happy. I am a girl/boy (child chooses). You are my friend. This is my teacher. I am fine.* Then question bridges assembled as whole chunks: *What's / your / name?* and *How / are / you?*
Error handling ("I happy" attempt — child taps GO with the plank missing): pirate steps, wobbles at the gap, hops back safely (comic, not scary). Parrot flies in holding the AM plank: **"I ... AM ... happy! You need *am*!"** — plank slots in glowing, full sentence replays, child taps GO again. (Model-and-parrot, explicit for this high-value error, per the research.)
Readers mode shows a one-time Hebrew hint card: "באנגלית תמיד צריך גשר: am / is / are — גם כשבעברית אין מילה כזאת." Pre-Readers rely on the visual metaphor alone.
Adjective-order guard: distractor tiles allow "happy I am" attempts → tiles gently sort themselves once, showing the fixed order, then a fresh retry.

### Level 6 — "Treasure Dive" (Spaced Review)
90-second dive. Bubbles float up containing due SRS items from Island 0 + this island (mixed types: hear→tap picture, picture→choose audio, Readers: word→picture). Relaxed pacing — bubbles pop only when answered, no timer pressure; the 90 seconds is a content budget, not a countdown shown to the child. Rusty (decayed) items appear as barnacled treasure that "polishes" when answered.

### Level 7 — BOSS: "Talk to Captain Morgan" (Guided Dialogue)
See §5 for full script. Passing = completing the dialogue (any path, any number of retries) + it triggers shield evaluation. Speaking is invited on every turn (rungs 1–2 of the ladder) but tap-response always suffices.

## 5. Boss Dialogue — Full Script

Format: **M** = Captain Morgan (recorded native speaker, warm, slow-normal speed, big gestures). Child responds by tapping one of 2–3 response cards (picture + audio on tap; + text in Readers mode), then is invited to say it: "🎤 Now you say it!" — recording plays back next to the model; constrained ASR (match against the known target phrase) awards 1–3 bonus stars for any recognizable attempt. Skip-mic always visible, no penalty.

| Turn | Captain Morgan | Response options (✔ = advances) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Ahoy! Hello!" 👋 | ✔ "Hello!" · ✔ "Hi!" · "Goodbye!" (Morgan chuckles: "No no, not yet! Hello!") | wrong option = gentle recast + retry, still no penalty |
| 2 | "What's your name?" | ✔ "My name is ___" · ✔ "I'm ___" | child's own name inserted from profile; TTS-free — the blank is the child's spoken/typed name; card audio says "My name is..." with a chime for the name slot |
| 3 | "Nice to meet you, ___!" (name via recorded template + name sting) | ✔ "Nice to meet you!" | single option — modeling turn, low pressure |
| 4 | "How are you?" | ✔ "I'm fine, thank you!" · ✔ "I'm happy!" · ✔ "I'm sad." | all three advance; if "sad," Morgan responds with empathy: "Oh! Here is a cookie. Are you happy now?" → "Yes! Thank you!" (extra turn, extra practice, kids adore it) |
| 5 | Morgan points at the parrot: "Who is this?" | ✔ "This is my friend!" | parrot preens |
| 6 | "Welcome to Name Island! Goodbye!" | ✔ "Goodbye!" · ✔ "Bye!" | confetti, shields ceremony, 25 coins + 1 pearl |

Branch memory: on replay, Morgan's turn order and some phrasings shuffle ("Good morning!" opener if played before 12:00 device time) so replays stay fresh and children can't pattern-match positions.

## 6. Mode Differences Summary

| Element | Pre-Readers | Readers |
|---|---|---|
| Level 3 | Sound Match (3B) | Word Builder (3A) |
| Response cards | picture + tap-to-hear audio | picture + English text + audio |
| Instructions | parrot demo animation + icons | Hebrew text + icons |
| Hebrew hint cards | none (visual metaphor only) | 1 per island max |
| Boss | identical script, picture cards | identical script, text on cards |

## 8. Trading Post Stock (this island)

Unlocked by docking: red/blue/green pirate hats and boots (color preview — colors formally taught next island, sold here by picture), parrot bandana, "Welcome" doormat for the home island. Purchase dialogue uses this island's chunks: shopkeeper says "Hello! ... Thank you! Goodbye!" — buying requires tapping/saying "Hello", the item card, "Please", closing with "Thank you". Pearl item: a talking figurehead that says "Good morning!" when the child opens the app.

## 9. Analytics & Adjustment Hooks (per research triggers)

- copula-inclusion rate in Level 5 and boss (target ≥80% by island end; below → auto-schedule a "Bridge" mini-replay next session)
- ASR attempt rate and retry/skip rate on boss turns (skip >40% → loosen threshold island-wide)
- per-level completion time and abandon points (Level 4 two-step commands are the predicted difficulty spike)
- SRS retention of the 18 items at day-7 (below expectation → shorten intervals game-wide, per research trigger (a))
